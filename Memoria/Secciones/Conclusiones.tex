\chapter{Conclusiones}
\label{chap:conclusiones}

% MOTIVACION

Cada vez es más conocido el peligro de los modelos de Inteligencia Artificial (IA) para suplantar identidades, de forma maliciosa, replicando la voz de una persona. A esto se le conoce como $deepfake$. La proliferación de estos modelos de IA para replicar la voz han venido de la mano de un detrimento de la capacidad de los humanos en diferenciar si una voz es real o no.

Esto puede comprometer un sinfín de sectores, ya que el mundo aún no ha adoptado las medidas necesarias para combatir estos fraudes, debido a la rápida evolución de estos. Es por estos factores que es crucial desarrollar una forma de comprobar si una voz fue manipulada o, en cambio, es real. 

En este Trabajo de Fin de Grado se propone el uso de Inteligencia Artificial para detectar la generación de voz falsa generada por modelos de IA. Además, como no podemos confiar plenamente en las decisiones de un modelo de IA, es imprescindible usar métodos de Inteligencia Artificial Explicable (XAI). Estos métodos muestran en qué se basan los modelos para tomar una decisión, permitiendo así que un humano interprete fácilmente las decisiones de la IA.

% RESUMEN

Para resolver el problema de la suplantación de voz mediante modelos de IA hemos extraído las características inherentes de la voz, como son sus formantes, espectrogramas, tono, entre otros. Estas características se han usado para entrenar modelos de IA, para que estos aprendan a diferenciar entre voces reales y voces clonadas. Después, se han aplicado técnicas de XAI para interpretar los resultados y poder decidir si podemos fiarnos de las clasificaciones hechas por la IA. Por último, se han tomado los modelos preentrentados y se ha hecho un ejemplo de cómo funcionarían en un caso real cuando reciben audios nuevos para clasificar. Además, se les han aplicado las mismas técnicas de XAI para interpretar los resultados.

% CONCLUSIONES

Tras terminar de revisar todo el proceso, desde la introducción del problema hasta las pruebas con modelos y una pequeña prueba real, se puede apreciar que el tema de esta investigación no es para nada trivial, pues diferentes modelos tienen diferentes resultados. Se pueden ver claras diferencias entre los modelos específicos para la voz frente a los modelos generales (los que entrenan con datos tabulares). Los generales parecen tener cierta predisposición por algunas características, es decir, les otorgan una alta importancia frente al resto de características. Si bien esto puede traducirse en una alta precisión dentro del entorno de entrenamiento, introduce una vulnerabilidad crítica: el riesgo de ``overfitting'' y la falta de robustez ante sutiles variaciones en la señal que resultarían imperceptibles para el oído humano. Por otro lado, los modelos específicos para el procesamiento de audio han demostrado un comportamiento más equilibrado y moderado. Esto no implica un rendimiento inferior, al contrario, sugiere una mayor capacidad de generalización al capturar mejor la naturaleza intrínseca de la voz, lo que los convierte en candidatos más estables y fiables para entornos de producción reales donde la variabilidad del entorno es la norma.  

En esta prueba se ha visto que es de vital importancia los audios con los que se entrenan los modelos. Esto es debido a que si se quiere clasificar un audio nuevo, y este fue grabado en otras condiciones los valores de las características serán muy distintos de aquellos con los que el modelo entrenó, haciendo así que se equivoque clasificándolo. Así que, un buen dataset con diversidad de condiciones y voces es necesario para un correcto funcionamiento de los modelos.

En definitiva, este trabajo no solo alcanza los objetivos planteados, sino que constituye una base sólida y viable para el desarrollo y la aplicación de técnicas de IA explicable sobre sistemas de detección de ``deepfakes'' de voz, además de abrir la puerta a futuras extensiones de los mismos.

\chapter*{Conclusions}
\label{chap:conclusiones}

% MOTIVATION

The danger posed by Artificial Intelligence (AI) models in maliciously impersonating individuals by replicating their voices is becoming increasingly well-known. This phenomenon is known as a \textit{deepfake}. The proliferation of these voice-cloning AI models has gone hand in hand with a detriment to the ability of humans to differentiate whether a voice is real or not.

This can compromise countless sectors, as the world has not yet adopted the necessary measures to combat these frauds due to their rapid evolution. Because of these factors, it is crucial to develop a way to verify whether a voice has been manipulated or, conversely, is genuine. 

This Bachelor's Thesis proposes the use of Artificial Intelligence to detect fake voices generated by AI models. Furthermore, since we cannot fully rely on the decisions of an AI model, it is essential to employ Explainable Artificial Intelligence (XAI) methods. These methods reveal what the models base their decisions on, thereby allowing a human to easily interpret the AI's predictions.

% ABSTRACT

To address the problem of voice spoofing via AI models, we have extracted the inherent features of speech, such as formants, spectrograms, and pitch, among others. These features were used to train AI models, enabling them to learn how to differentiate between genuine and cloned voices. Subsequently, XAI techniques were applied to interpret the results and determine whether the classifications made by the AI can be trusted. Finally, the pre-trained models were integrated into a pipeline to simulate how they would perform in a real-world scenario when receiving new audio samples for classification. Additionally, the same XAI techniques were applied to interpret these new results.

% CONCLUSIONS

After reviewing the entire pipeline, from the introduction of the problem to the model evaluations and a small real-world test, it is evident that this research topic is far from trivial, as different models yield different results. Clear distinctions can be observed between voice-specific models and general classifiers (those trained on tabular data). General models seem to have a certain predisposition toward specific features, meaning they assign them a high importance compared to the remaining features. While this can translate into high accuracy within the training environment, it introduces a critical vulnerability: the risk of overfitting and a lack of robustness against subtle variations in the signal that would be imperceptible to the human ear. On the other hand, models specifically designed for audio processing have demonstrated a more balanced and moderated behavior. This does not imply inferior performance; on the contrary, it suggests a greater capacity for generalization by better capturing the intrinsic nature of speech, making them more stable and reliable candidates for real production environments where environmental variability is the norm.

This testing phase has shown that the audio samples used to train the models are of vital importance. This is because if one wishes to classify a new audio file that was recorded under different conditions, the feature values will differ significantly from those the model was trained on, causing it to misclassify the sample. Therefore, a comprehensive dataset featuring a wide diversity of acoustic conditions and voices is necessary for the proper functioning of the models.

In short, this thesis not only achieves the proposed objectives but also establishes a solid and viable foundation for the development and application of explainable AI techniques to voice deepfake detection systems, while opening the door to future extensions of this work.

\section*{Future Work}

The work developed establishes a solid foundation for the detection of audio deepfakes through the use of different AI methods and XAI techniques.
Currently, the final outcome of the work consists of a pipeline that makes it possible to apply, to an input audio, all the classification and explanation models studied in chapter~\ref{sec:metodologia}. This pipeline produces as output the results obtained by the different AI models, combined with graphs resulting from the application of XAI to those results.

Although these representations make it possible to observe which factors have influenced the models' decisions, their interpretation requires a certain level of technical knowledge, and the explanation is not always sufficiently direct. Therefore, as future work, the development of an explainer is proposed, acting as a final layer between the values computed by the XAI techniques applied in the pipeline and the final result shown.
The main idea would be to transform the current system, which is focused on visualization, into an approach based on assisted interpretability. That is, the system would not be limited to presenting graphs, but would also provide more easily interpretable explanations regarding the final decision and the evidence supporting this classification.

Furthermore, two types of explainers are suggested, which could be complementary to one another. The first would be based on \textbf{large language models, or LLMs}. The objective of this first explainer would be to convert technical outputs into more comprehensible and synthesized textual explanations. This approach presents several advantages. First, it would allow non-expert users to understand the reason behind the final classification. Second, it would facilitate comparison between the results of the different models.
Third, it would make it possible to generate automatic reports summarizing the main evidence used for the classification of the audio.

The second type of proposed explainer would be based on \textbf{counterfactuals}. Whereas XAI techniques mainly answer which parts or features have influenced the final decision, counterfactuals answer a different question: what would need to change in the audio for its classification to be different?
This type of explanation is especially interesting because it also makes it possible to analyze the sensitivity of the models to specific modifications of the input.

This type of explainer could indicate the minimum alteration that causes the models to change the original classification of the audio. Depending on the type of modification, three possibilities are considered:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Counterfactuals based on temporal segments}: the system could modify, hide, or replace specific segments of the audio and observe how the predictions change.
    \item \textbf{Counterfactuals based on spectrograms}: this approach would operate in the same way as the previous one, but by performing modifications on the spectral representation of the audio.
    \item \textbf{Counterfactuals based on acoustic features}: the explainer would modify acoustic features such as MFCCs or pitch, with the aim of determining whether the models' outputs are altered.
\end{itemize}

However, this proposal contains limitations that must be considered. First, it is necessary to guarantee the fidelity of the explanations with respect to the actual behavior of the models. Second, traceability must be ensured; that is, each statement generated by the explainer must be linkable to a specific output of the pipeline. Third, counterfactuals must be realistic and must not consist of modifications that fall outside the coherence of the audio domain.

In conclusion, the development of an explainer module would improve the scope of the developed system, shifting from a pipeline capable of detecting and displaying evidence in the form of graphs to a tool capable of justifying its results in a clearer and more interpretable manner. This evolution would contribute to improving the transparency of the classification process, facilitate the analysis of audio recordings, and increase the usefulness of the system in contexts where the aim is not only to know the classification of an audio recording, but also to understand what evidence led to that decision.
